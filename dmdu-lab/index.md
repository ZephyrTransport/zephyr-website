## Why this initiative exists

Every travel forecast rests on assumptions &mdash; about fuel prices, telework rates, vehicle
automation, land use, the economy. Traditional "predict-then-act" planning treats those
assumptions as fixed. Decision Making under Deep Uncertainty (DMDU) starts from the opposite
premise: assumptions will be wrong in ways we can't fully anticipate, so plans should be built
and tested against a wide range of futures, not just one.

The initiative continues the work started under the TRB AEP50 Travel Forecasting Committee's
Uncertainty/DMDU initiative, with four objectives:

- Promote a broad understanding of practical approaches to effectively inform decision-makers
  faced with uncertainty underpinning baseline assumptions in travel forecasting and planning
- Advance the state of the practice in the specification, development, and application of
  appropriate quantitative tools to support DMDU
- Advance DMDU adoption inside planning organizations
- Continue the work of the TRB Travel Forecasting DMDU initiative

## Our Goals

We are modelers, so this initiative takes a model-centric view of DMDU &mdash; but a big part of
DMDU is moving the focus away from model forecasts and towards getting to better decisions.

- Complement predictive applications of models with insights generated from exploratory
  applications
- Promote use of models in concert with decision makers and stakeholders
- Facilitate an adaptive planning process where multiple futures are considered and immediate
  steps are taken to maintain flexibility and resiliency
